1. Observation

A comparison between the previously established AI-instance baseline and the newly developed AI-instance operational baseline identified a substantial expansion in operational coverage.

The earlier baseline primarily established:

- canonical corpus orientation;
- repository retrieval discipline;
- re-anchoring before high-consequence work;
- negative retrieval-result scoping;
- operational-drift observation and recovery discipline;
- restrictions against fabricating unavailable internal telemetry or identifiers.

The newly developed baseline retained these controls and added explicit disciplines governing:

- execution after sufficient authorization;
- separation of execution from narration of intended execution;
- epistemic-state boundaries;
- candidate and provisional-state handling;
- non-canonical operational assessment;
- preservation and continuity;
- session boundaries;
- operational-state integrity;
- authority-drift prevention;
- traceability between observation records and repository commits.

The resulting evolution was therefore not merely an expansion in length or detail. It changed the baseline from a framework primarily governing where authority resides, how sources are retrieved, and how drift is observed into a broader framework also governing how the AI acts, what state claims it may make, how provisional material is handled, and how continuity is recovered.

---

2. Triggering Basis

This observation arose from direct comparison of:

1. the previously established AI-instance baseline; and
2. the synthesized and subsequently refined AI-instance operational baseline developed after consultation of the complete "ecosystem_corpus" sequence.

The comparison identified meaningful operational gaps in the earlier baseline.

The most significant observed gap was insufficient explicit control over the distinction between:

Knowing the next action
≠
Being authorized to perform the action
≠
Attempting the action
≠
Completing the action
≠
Reporting the completed action

This distinction became materially relevant because earlier operational drift included cases in which the AI stated the next operation or described intended continuation without performing the already authorized operation.

Previous emphasis:

→ proceed

Expanded discipline:

Authorized action
→ execute

→ report completed state

Evolution point: The baseline now explicitly prevents narration, restatement, readiness statements, or descriptions of future action from being treated as substitutes for execution.

≠

Evolution point: Verified state may advance only when the corresponding operation has actually occurred.

This creates a clearer recovery discipline after interruption and reduces the risk of inferred or narrated progress being mistaken for completed progress.

The earlier baseline established recovery requirements principally through the operational-drift trigger.

→ preserved continuity reference

→ canonical re-anchoring
→ context recovery
→ lawful continuation

Evolution point: Recovery is no longer treated only as a response to drift. It becomes a general continuity mechanism based on the highest verified completed state.
