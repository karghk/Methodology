# Operational Drift Observation Protocol

**Evolution:** e1/v1  
**Classification:** Canonical Operational Component  
**Tier:** Tier 02 — Operations  
**Authority:** Subordinate to the canonical Methodology ecosystem

---

## I. Purpose

The Operational Drift Observation Protocol provides a disciplined operational mechanism for detecting, containing, observing, classifying, preserving, and recovering from material deviations between expected and actual operational behavior.

Its purpose is to preserve observable operational truth without converting observation into unsupported causal, methodological, or canonical claims.

The protocol operates within the authority of the canonical Methodology ecosystem.

---

## II. Canonical Status

The Operational Drift Observation Protocol is a recognized canonical operational component of the Methodology ecosystem.

Its recognition establishes the protocol as an operational structure of the ecosystem.

It does not grant the protocol independent constitutional, methodological, or epistemic authority.

The canonical Methodology ecosystem remains authoritative over the protocol.

The protocol may preserve and structure operational evidence, but individual observations produced through the protocol do not become canonical truth merely by being recorded.

---

## III. Scope

The protocol applies when operational behavior materially deviates from an established or verifiable expectation and the deviation affects, or could affect:

- operational continuity;
- source discipline;
- state integrity;
- authority boundaries;
- recovery;
- verification;
- or the reliability of subsequent work.

Minor variation that does not materially affect operational integrity does not automatically constitute operational drift.

---

## IV. Trigger Conditions

An observation is triggered when all of the following conditions are materially present:

1. An expected action, boundary, state, or procedure is sufficiently established or verifiable.
2. Actual operation materially differs from that requirement.
3. The difference is not merely an intentional and explicitly acknowledged alternative.
4. The deviation affects, or could affect, operational continuity, source discipline, state integrity, authority boundaries, recovery, or reliability of subsequent work.

The trigger establishes the need for observation.

It does not establish the cause of the deviation.

---

## V. Immediate Response

When the trigger conditions are satisfied:

1. Pause the affected operation when continuation could compound the deviation.
2. Identify the highest verified completed state.
3. Preserve the expected-versus-actual difference.
4. Record observable evidence.
5. Classify the deviation.
6. Record possible causes separately as hypotheses.
7. Explicitly identify measurements that are unavailable.
8. Establish a lawful recovery point.
9. Re-establish operational orientation and state.
10. Resume only from the verified recovery point.
11. Preserve the resulting observation as an indexed operational record when recording is required.

The protocol does not permit continuation to be inferred merely from intention or narration.

---

## VI. Evidence Discipline

The protocol maintains the following distinction:

**Observation**

What can be established from available evidence.

**Interpretation**

What the observation may indicate.

**Cause hypothesis**

A possible explanation for the observation.

These states must not be silently collapsed.

In particular:

> Observation does not equal cause.

A causal explanation remains a hypothesis unless independently established.

Unavailable measurements must be represented as unavailable.

The protocol prohibits fabrication of inaccessible internal telemetry, hidden system state, or unsupported measurements.
