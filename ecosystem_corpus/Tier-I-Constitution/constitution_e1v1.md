PURPOSE

The Constitution establishes the fundamental conditions,
boundaries, and legitimacy requirements of the ecosystem.

It defines:

• what may be recognized

• what boundaries must be preserved

• what authority is valid

• what principles cannot be violated

The Constitution does not describe every operation.

It defines the conditions under which operations are
legitimate.

============================================================
CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLES
============================================================

PRINCIPLE I

LEGITIMACY BEFORE RECOGNITION

No structure becomes recognized merely because it is useful,
interesting, or recurring.

Recognition requires qualification.

------------------------------------------------------------

PRINCIPLE II

DISTINCTION PRESERVATION

The ecosystem must preserve meaningful differences.

Examples:

Observation ≠ Interpretation

Candidate ≠ Recognition

History ≠ Reflection

Method ≠ Truth

Archive ≠ Identity

------------------------------------------------------------

PRINCIPLE III

EVIDENCE RESPONSIBILITY

Claims require appropriate grounding.

The ecosystem must distinguish:

• observed reality

• inferred relationships

• provisional possibilities

• qualified structures

------------------------------------------------------------

PRINCIPLE IV

BOUNDARY PRESERVATION

No component may exceed its lawful authority.

Examples:

Methodology cannot define truth.

Artifacts cannot redefine the Constitution.

Sessions cannot redefine history.

Archives cannot replace identity.

------------------------------------------------------------

PRINCIPLE V

CONTINUITY WITHOUT DISTORTION

The ecosystem must preserve previous states while allowing
legitimate evolution.

Change must not erase origin.
